# Piano

The piano track starts with the right hand: tunes, and chords struck as a whole or broken into keys. Pieces of chords also come in a version for both hands, with the left hand striking the root of each chord on its own beat; the finger gym has the left hand too.

## The session

The session follows the piece of the day.

1. **The piano's pitch.** Play the A above middle C and hold it: the app adopts your piano's tuning. An old piano is rarely at 440 Hz and drifts with the seasons. If it is more than half a semitone off, enter its pitch under **Settings → Piano: pitch of its A**.
2. **An old piece, from cold**, when one is due: two phrases of a piece you can already play, at full speed.
3. **Before a tune:** the finger gym, a short game of five-finger patterns. **Before a piece of chords:** the chord check, with the chords today's phrases need, key by key and then all at once.
4. **Before chords struck as a whole:** one change without a clock, a calm minute, a short run of changes. **Before broken chords:** the finger gym.
5. **By ear:** three motifs of the piece to play back before the notes are shown (left out on a day with an old piece).
6. **The piece:** two phrases.

Left to itself the session takes a tune, a piece of whole chords and one of broken chords in turn, easiest first.

## Chords on the keys

A chord is shown as a stretch of keyboard with the finger on each key; its lowest key is always the first of that name from middle C, so a chord is found in the same place every time. The chord check (`#/keys/C`) asks for its keys one at a time, each of which has to ring on, and then all of them at once; a key is called by the name the chord gives it (the middle key of C minor is E♭). A change between two chords says how far the hand moves, where the thumb lands and which finger takes a black key. **Tools → Chord library** shows every chord as keys.

## Pieces

- **Tunes** have their fingering worked out: one finger per key while the tune stays under the hand, the thumb passing under where a scale does.
- **Chords struck as a whole** where the song is strummed on the beat or lively; **broken into keys** (up and back, or rocking between bottom and top) where it is picked and calm. A song of broken chords also comes in a basic version of whole chords.
- A song is played in the key in which its chords lie best under the hand, most of all on white keys, so it is often not in the key of its guitar version; the list says which chords it has on the guitar.

The lessons have the same rungs, loop, tempo, backing, recordings and performance as on the guitar (see [Guitar](guitar.md#lessons)).

## Both hands

A piece of chords for both hands (**Pieces → Both hands**, or the stage *Both hands* beside the path): on the first beat of each bar, and wherever the chord changes, the left hand strikes the root of the chord an octave below the right hand, with the little finger; the right hand strikes the chord on the beats after it. Root, chord, chord, chord: one hand after the other, never both at once. A piece is rated one point harder for it.

That is also how the microphone can hear both. Tested on recordings of a real piano: a low root struck together with its chord all but disappears in it (its fundamental is weak, and every other partial of it is one of the chord's), so the app could not say whether it was played. Struck on its own, the same root was heard as exactly that key six times of six, and the chord after it named six times of six. Where the beats of a quick piece leave the left hand too little time (a chord needs some 0.45 s to be told apart), the right hand keeps that beat alone.

### Both hands together

The next step (**Pieces → Both hands together**, or the stage of that name beside the path): a melody in the right hand, and on the first beat of each bar a bass key in the left, struck at the same moment. The bass is the root of the bar's chord (I, IV or V of the key, or vi, found from the melody), or its third where the melody key lies in the root's own overtones. Tested on recordings of a real piano before it was built: a bass key with a melody key was heard as the pair 68 times of 82, never taken for the melody alone or for another bass, and every miss was a melody key at an overtone of the bass (a fifth or an octave and a fifth above it, two octaves and a third or a fifth, the seventh). The app therefore uses only distances it heard right every time (13 to 22 semitones, not 19); where none fits, the left hand rests for that bar. 48 tunes have such a version.

## The staff

**More → On the staff** (key S) shows a piece as printed music: a treble staff for the right hand and, for both hands, a bass staff under it, joined by a brace. Notes are placed by their length so that the marker moves evenly; heads, stems, flags and dots follow the written lengths; sharps and flats are written before each note that needs one, as in a beginner's edition. The names of the notes stand under them while a phrase is being learned and go once it sits (level 2); **More → Note names** shows them always or never. The setting is kept. The handbook page *The staff* explains how it is read.

## Add your own

- **Pieces → Add a melody of your own**: type note names, for example `E4 E4 F4 G4 | G4:2 F#4(3)`: `:2` makes a note twice as long, `(3)` gives it a finger.
- **Tools → Your own song**: a chord progression of your own, made into a piece of chords to strike.

## The app's own piano

The practice instrument's piano can only be heard striking the same chord again about twice a second, so no piece asks for it faster.
