# Piano

The piano track is for the right hand: tunes, and chords struck as a whole or broken into keys. A microphone cannot tell which hand struck which key, so both hands together are left out; the finger gym has the left hand too.

## The session

The session follows the piece of the day.

1. **The piano's pitch.** Play the A above middle C and hold it: the app adopts your piano's tuning. An old piano is rarely at 440 Hz and drifts with the seasons. If it is more than half a semitone off, enter its pitch under **Settings → Piano: pitch of its A**.
2. **Before a tune:** the finger gym, a short game of five-finger patterns. **Before a piece of chords:** the chord check, with the chords today's phrases need, key by key and then all at once.
3. **Before chords struck as a whole:** one change without a clock, a calm minute, a short run of changes. **Before broken chords:** the finger gym.
4. **The piece:** two phrases.

Left to itself the session takes a tune, a piece of whole chords and one of broken chords in turn, easiest first.

## Chords on the keys

A chord is shown as a stretch of keyboard with the finger on each key; its lowest key is always the first of that name from middle C, so a chord is found in the same place every time. The chord check (`#/keys/C`) asks for its keys one at a time, each of which has to ring on, and then all of them at once; a key is called by the name the chord gives it (the middle key of C minor is E♭). A change between two chords says how far the hand moves, where the thumb lands and which finger takes a black key.

## Pieces

- **Tunes** have their fingering worked out: one finger per key while the tune stays under the hand, the thumb passing under where a scale does. A tune is shown on a note strip read left to right, with the finger number on each key.
- **Chords struck as a whole** where the song is strummed on the beat or lively; **broken into keys** (up and back, or rocking between bottom and top) where it is picked and calm. A song of broken chords also comes in a basic version of whole chords.
- A song is played in the key in which its chords lie best under the hand, most of all on white keys, so it is often not in the key of its guitar version; the list says which chords it has on the guitar.

The lessons have the same three rungs as on the guitar (see [Guitar](guitar.md#lessons)).

## Add your own

**Pieces → Add a melody of your own**: type note names, for example `E4 E4 F4 G4 | G4:2 F#4(3)`: `:2` makes a note twice as long, `(3)` gives it a finger.

## The app's own piano

The practice instrument's piano can only be heard striking the same chord again about twice a second, so no piece asks for it faster.
